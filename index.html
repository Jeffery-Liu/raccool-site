A document-thriller · PC & Steam Deck

You are the grader.
The number on the label is yours.

A trading-card grading house in Vancouver. Thirty days. Every card comes across your desk, and you tilt it under the lamp to find what it has survived — hairline scratches, pressure dents, print lines — visible only at the angle that catches them.

You decide what the card is and what it is worth: a 1–10 grade, or no grade at all. Under quota. While the company bills the customer more when your number goes higher.

On Day 9 the second grader who taught you the job starts changing your grades. On Day 30 a card you refused to grade turns up sealed with a 10 and your name on the file. The last day is your hearing, not your shift.

01

Every verdict is derivable

The right grade is computed from the card in front of you, never rolled. If you were wrong, you can go back and see why.

02

The tilt is the game

Flaws live on the surface, not in a menu. A real light, a real angle, and a hand that learns where to look.

03

The record remembers

Two ledgers of every verdict — yours and the company's. The day they start to disagree is the day the story begins.
